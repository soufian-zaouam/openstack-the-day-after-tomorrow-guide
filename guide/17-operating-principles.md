# 17 · The operating principles

*The philosophy in one page*

**The book states its operating rules as principles. They are recommendations, not laws of nature; a different platform and organization may weigh them differently. Together they describe one way of thinking about production.**

![The book’s operating principles grouped under Visibility, Stability, Organization, Governance and Evolution.](../images/17-operating-principles.png)

### Visibility

> **The purpose of observability is to make the platform understandable enough to support the right decision at the right time.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 8</sub>

### Stability

> **Before changing a mission-critical OpenStack platform, understand enough to know what you are risking, how you will detect failure, and how you will recover if you are wrong.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 9</sub>

> **Stopping is not giving up control. In the right circumstances, it is how control is preserved.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 19</sub>

> **Recovery is a feature because a platform is not operational if the organization cannot demonstrate how it returns to a trusted state.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 10</sub>

### Organization

> **The measure of a successful deployment is whether the platform remains operable when the deployment experts are no longer present.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 1</sub>

> **A team is resilient when the platform remains operable after its experts change.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 12</sub>

> **Documentation is operational memory only when the organization has a deliberate way to create it, maintain it, test it, use it and retire it.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 11</sub>

### Governance

> **Everyone can challenge. Someone must decide.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 13</sub>

> **Complexity is part of the operational risk of an OpenStack platform, and it must earn its place.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 3</sub>

> **A workaround is safe only while the risk it introduces remains smaller than the risk it removes.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 15</sub>

### Evolution

> **Stay as close to upstream as practical.**<br>
> <sub>Introduction · Chapter 14</sub>

> **The objective is the right level of automation for the platform the organization can actually operate, which is rarely the maximum.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 16</sub>

> **Security requirements define what must be achieved. Platform capacity and business criticality determine how safely it can be achieved.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 20</sub>

> **A successful OpenStack evolution is a platform whose future can still be operated deliberately, not merely a newer one.**<br>
> <sub>Chapter 18</sub>
